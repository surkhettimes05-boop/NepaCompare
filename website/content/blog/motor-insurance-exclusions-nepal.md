An exclusion describes when a policy will not respond. It is as important as the coverage summary.

## Themes found in official insurer material

Shikhar’s vehicle page and FAQ identify examples including driving outside the stated geographical area, driving without a valid licence, driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs, contractual liability, use beyond stated limitations, ordinary mechanical wear and tear, and certain tyre-only damage.

These examples are not a universal or complete list. Your policy schedule, wording and endorsements control.

## How to review exclusions

1. Match the wording version to the policy period.
2. Read definitions before relying on a coverage heading.
3. Check driver, usage and geographical restrictions.
4. Find deductibles, depreciation and claim conditions.
5. Ask the insurer to clarify ambiguous language in writing.

Never treat a marketing page or comparison card as the complete contract.
